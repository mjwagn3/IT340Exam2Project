However, my partner implemented the project using a single file. While I believe my design

will explain how the implementation works, why the model is only achieving about 70% 
accuracy, and what changes could be made to improve its performance.

One of the biggest factors affecting the model's accuracy is the presence of filler words, 
also known as stop words. Examples include words such as "the," "a," "an," and many other
common words. These words frequently appear in both ham and spam emails, but they provide 
very little useful information about whether an email is spam.

For example, consider the following spam emails:

- "You are the winner, claim your free prize."
- "You are the best, but only if you send the money."

Now consider these ham emails:

- "Can you send me the reports?"
- "The weather outside is terrible. Are you going to be okay?"

In both spam and ham emails, words such as "the," "are," and "you" appear regularly. Because 
these words occur in both categories, they do not help the classifier determine whether an 
email is spam or ham. In contrast, words such as "winner," "free," "prize," and "money" are more 
strongly associated with spam emails and provide useful information for classification.

As a result, including large numbers of filler words in the calculations can introduce noise into the model. While individual stop words may not significantly harm the classifier, collectively they can reduce the impact of more informative words and contribute to lower overall accuracy. Removing these words before training the model could improve performance and help the classifier focus on the words that are most useful for distinguishing spam from ham emails.
